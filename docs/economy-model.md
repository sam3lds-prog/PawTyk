# PawTyk economy model (D51-B)

A configurable model of how Paw Coins, extra lives, hints, food and the two premium currencies flow for a player,
read from the game's **real** numbers: `src/config/economy.ts`, the decoration, trick, food and bowl catalogs, the
errand pool, the pantry rules and each home's completion gift. It runs three play patterns (conservative, base,
higher engagement). Code: `src/meta/economyModel.ts` (pure, tested in `test/unit/d50Validation.test.ts`). Report:
`npm run economy:model` (add `-- --write` to refresh the block below).

**It changes nothing in the game.** It exists so that any economy change is argued from numbers. Until the beta runs,
the play patterns are assumptions. Each one names the opt-in analytics event that will replace it (see the
`EconomyScenario` fields and `docs/analytics.md`).

## What the D50/D51 plan rules out, whatever the model shows

- Cutting free extra lives to sell revives. First measure where boards are lost and whether players retry
  (`puzzle_fail`, `puzzle_retry`, `revive_offer`).
- Paid streak protection. It turns a pleasant daily habit into avoiding a penalty.
- A VIP pass, or "Remove Ads" sold for little value. PawTyk shows no forced ads. Existing purchasers keep their
  entitlement.
- Renaming the bundle id or any App Store product id (`pawtopia.gems.*`, `pawtopia.removeads`,
  `pawtyk.welcomepack`). They can't be renamed once created without a migration plan.
- Big gem sinks (a 1,500-gem target) made only to create demand. Make premium content appealing first.

## Reading it

- **Coins come mostly from playing new levels.** First clears are about half of every pattern's coins. Errands are
  the next biggest source. Food is small: about 5–6% of what a player earns, at the median portion price.
- **The first Farm piece the animals use is within a day's play** in every pattern. This is the moment D50's
  Featured now points at.
- **Everything sold for coins is a long goal, not a wall:** weeks for a conservative player, days for an engaged one.
  Anything new for coins should keep that shape (D48 measured new items in levels played; this measures in days).
- **Extra lives and hints are earned faster than they are used**, in every pattern. The pantry caps at 10 of each,
  and anything over the cap arrives as coins. So today a player rarely runs short, and there is no case for selling
  revives. If the beta shows lives piling up unused, that is a sign boards are comfortable, not that lives should
  be cut.
- **Video tokens:** the dearest token item takes about eight days at one video every other day (base), and is never reached
  without videos (conservative). That is fine for a fully optional, cosmetic tier. Don't lower the daily cap to
  push demand.
- **Gems** are only for premium cosmetics. The model doesn't predict purchases: D51-C (a cosmetic pilot) waits for
  real preview and purchase data (`product_view`, `trick_tried`, `featured_tap`, `iap_success`).

## Replacing the assumptions with the beta's numbers

1. Turn on opt-in analytics in the beta build (`VITE_FIREBASE_ANALYTICS=1`; see `docs/analytics.md`).
2. After two weeks, read each `EconomyScenario` field from its event: the 25th, 50th and 75th percentile players
   make new conservative, base and engaged patterns.
3. Put them in `SCENARIOS` and run `npm run economy:model -- --write`. Commit the doc with the numbers it came from.
4. Opt-in players aren't a fair sample. Pair the numbers with observed TestFlight sessions before acting on them.
